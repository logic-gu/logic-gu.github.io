In this talk, our main goal is to show how to provide a uniform, purely syntactic, and proof-theoretic treatment of a wide range of logics: starting from S5, we then move to its multi-agent version, reach Public Announcement Logic (PAL), and finally conclude (by hinting how to treat the case of) Dynamic Epistemic Logic (DEL). To achieve our goal, we will use hypersequents -- generalizations of classical Gentzen-style calculus -- which will first be extended to cover the multi-agent case, and then become dynamic hypersequents to capture the dynamism inherent to PAL and DEL. We will show how each calculus can be obtained through changes to the logical rules while structural rules always remain admissible, including contraction rules. Every calculus is sound and complete, and satisfies the cut-elimination theorem. We will conclude the talk with some general observations and indications of future paths of research. The talk is partly based on a joint work with C. Lerouvillois
